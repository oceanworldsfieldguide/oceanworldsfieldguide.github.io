The Ocean Worlds Field Guide

The Ocean Worlds Field Guide#

Edited by Marggy Chiviri, Chris German, Shannon MacKenzie, Jacqueline M. Grebmeier, Alison E. Murray. Published by Willow Houck, Dwan Jackson, and Patrick Gray. First Edition: November 2026

The Ocean Worlds Field Guide (OWFG) was envisioned several years ago by the co-leads of NASA’s Research Coordination Network, the Network for Ocean Worlds (NOW). The goal was to create a broad, accessible overview of the emerging field of Ocean Worlds Science and Technology—one that would serve as a useful resource for both newcomers and those already actively engaged in the field.

Ocean Worlds research is inherently interdisciplinary and collaborative. It brings together planetary scientists who investigate geophysics, geochemistry, and planetary evolution, as well as those who conduct remote astronomical observations using ground-based and space-based telescopes or instruments aboard spacecraft missions. These scientists work alongside oceanographers, geologists, microbiologists, and technologists whose research focuses on Earth’s oceans. Their areas of expertise include the influence of hydrothermal vents on ocean geochemistry, the role of Earth’s cryosphere in ocean processes, the distribution and diversity of life in marine environments, the use of spectroscopic observations to study ocean systems, and the detection of even the smallest forms of life in ocean environments, to name just a few.

The Network for Ocean Worlds provides a natural forum for these diverse communities to connect, exchange ideas, and develop new collaborations. It is also a place where technology developers and instrument builders can engage directly with scientists working across these disciplines. Such interactions can inspire the next generation of science and technology missions, advancing our understanding of ocean worlds and expanding our ability to study them both remotely and in situ. Ultimately, these efforts will help us better understand the habitability of ocean worlds, their chemical environments, and the potential for signs of life beyond Earth.

Given the breadth of scientific and engineering disciplines represented in Ocean Worlds research, this Field Guide is was written with the goal of providing an accessible, entry-level resource that provides foundational background and introduces emerging areas of study. It is designed to facilitate communication across disciplines, offering readers both new to the field and those already well-versed in particular aspects of Ocean Worlds research an opportunity to broaden their understanding and appreciate the connections between these diverse areas of inquiry.

The Ocean Worlds Field Guide grew out of a retreat of the Network for Ocean Worlds Steering Committee and the NOW early career cohort, Future Leaders for Ocean Worlds (FLOW), along with invited participants from other NASA Research Coordination Networks. The retreat was held in August 2023 at the Wrigley Marine Science Research Institute on Catalina Island, California. The scientific and technical presentations were organized into eight thematic modules spanning the breadth of Ocean Worlds research. These modules were designed to bring the community together, share expertise, and create opportunities for participants to learn from one another:

  • Module 1: Habitability on Ocean Worlds and on Earth

  • Module 2: Biogeochemical Cycles on Earth and Ocean Worlds

  • Module 3: Strategies for Life Detection

  • Module 4: Ocean Processes on Earth and Ocean Worlds

  • Module 5: Cryosphere Processes on Earth Relevant to Ocean Worlds

  • Module 6: The Coupling Between Ice and Ocean on Icy Satellites

  • Module 7: Missions and Technologies Between Earth and Planetary Sciences

  • Module 8: Experimental Science Informing Ocean Worlds

A ninth module was added as a collaborative team exercise in which participants worked together to identify suitable instrument packages for a potential mission to Ganymede. These nine modules, along with much of the scientific and technical content presented at the retreat, form the foundation of this Ocean Worlds Field Guide eBook.

The content of this EBook is intended to provide enough information to grasp the subject material – with points to follow up reading through the peer-reviewed scientific literature. Each Module of the EBook is structured in which the content is organized into three levels. Level 1 introduces the topic and provides a broad overview; Level 2 takes a deeper dive into different key areas that the topic addresses, focusing on current topics of interest in Ocean Worlds science; Level 3 takes the reader into specific technical insights and case study examples of the subject.

The OWFG is a living document, and will be edited on an approximately annual basis as new developments in the field emerge.

In addition to the contributions of the module authors, whose names are listed with their respective sections, we are especially grateful to Marggy Chiviri, a master’s student and NASA Headquarters intern in 2025, who served as the primary editor of the content across the various modules. Following additional editorial input from the NOW co-leads—Chris German, Shannon MacKenzie, Jackie Grebmeier, and Alison Murray—the eBook was drafted and assembled by NASA 2026 intern Dwan Jackson and NASA Research Coordination Network coordinator Willow Houck, under the guidance of Dr. Patrick Gray of the University of Maine. We acknowledge support by NASA for the Network for Ocean Worlds (NASA Awards 80NSSC24K0220 to AEM, the NOW retreat (22-TWSC22-00057), and NASA Awards 26-SMDSS26-0189 and NNH17ZDA003C to WHOI) in which the content of this EBook was prepared for.

We hope this Field Guide will serve as a valuable resource for the Ocean Worlds community, fostering interdisciplinary understanding, stimulating new collaborations, and inspiring the next generation of scientific discoveries and exploration of ocean worlds.
